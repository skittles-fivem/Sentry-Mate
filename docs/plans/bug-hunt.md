# Bug hunt

[← Back to the README](../../README.md)

The owner's instruction: find every problem and bug in every page and every feature, and assume everything is broken. This page records what was checked, what was found and fixed, and what was found and is still open. All data used was made up.

How it was done: the code was split into areas (below). In each round one hunter per area listed every feature, route, endpoint and job of its area and tried to break each one (edge cases, hostile input, concurrency, data correctness, every page at phone, laptop, 1440p and 4K sizes, light and dark, each interface size). A second pass then tried to refute every finding against the code and the docs; only what it could reproduce counted. Each confirmed bug got a failing test first where that was feasible, then the fix.

State: rounds 1 to 4 done, and the owner's reports from the real server in the same weeks. Round 2: its 41 earlier reports all confirmed and fixed but two kept as limits, 13 more found and fixed. Round 3: 18 found, 17 fixed, 1 kept. Round 4 (a review of every round-3 change and the flows no round had driven): 8 found, all fixed. Round 4 still found bugs, so by the owner's stop rule the hunt ends here with what it found fixed, not with a round that found nothing. From round 1: 144 bugs fixed, 2 found and open, 39 archived with their pack, 4 reports refuted. The owner's reports are all fixed but one in part: the full rebuild's cost a log on their server is lower now where it grew with the history, and its windows, which their server's Rebuild speed line showed taking 85 % of it, are built once per day of logs; what that gives there is read on their server.

## The owner's five decisions

Round 1 left five items for the owner. What was decided and where each stands:

1. A damaged table of a car: left as it is (the data_damaged notice, and the car's folder put back from a backup). Done: written down in the docs under Restore. Later, the drives table was made mendable (see Fixed, Background jobs and storage); other tables stay as decided.
2. A rebuild of a period as fast as a whole one: only with a proof that every id comes out the same. Done, tests compare everything stored, ids included, against a window per log. A period rebuilds per run of logs; where its end cuts a drive or a charge, the logs from an hour before that drive's start get a window each and the logs before, which carry none of its samples, go per run (a week's middle third, which ends 18 minutes into a charge: 236 windows after every log, 11.0 s, against 25, 2.1 s). Still per log where it cannot be told that the logs before are free of the drive (see Found and not fixed).
3. Light theme: every text, links included, at 4.5:1 or more. Done, with a test that measures every text on drawn pixels in both themes.
4. Interface size 125 on a phone: the map's chip never covers the car's marker. Done, with a test.
5. Example addresses: 192.0.2.x everywhere. Done, and the repo hygiene test keeps it so.

With them came three unsteady tests, each mended at its cause: the list of cars and a car's Now disagreeing on since when (the server's rule), the storage days of a far zone (the test's car zone), and a page that loads again on rows events (the page's timing).
